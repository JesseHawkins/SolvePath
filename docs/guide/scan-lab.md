# Scan Lab {#guide.scan-lab}

Scan Lab is an early test of reading a real cube with the camera. It's rough on purpose. We're using it to find out how well scanning works before we build the real scan screen.

## Scanning a cube {#feature.scan-lab}

Open **Scan Lab** at the top of the page and press **Turn on the camera**. Your browser will ask if SolvePath may use the camera. The video stays on your device: nothing is recorded, saved, or sent.

Press **Start scan**, then follow the words under the picture. The dashed box shows where to hold the cube. When we find a side, a grid appears on it. The grid is yellow while we're looking and turns green when the side is held still long enough to read.

The little speed number shows how long each picture takes to read. Lower is better.

## How to hold and spin the cube {#scan.grip}

1. Hold the cube with one finger on the middle of the top and your thumb on the middle of the bottom.
2. Point one side at the camera and hold still until the grid turns green.
3. Spin the cube a quarter turn between your fingers, always the same way, and hold still again. Do this until all four sides are read.
4. Tip the top toward the camera and hold still.
5. Tip the bottom toward the camera and hold still.

It's fine if you show a side twice: we notice and skip it. Your fingers can cover the middle stickers of the top and bottom. We work those out from the rest of the cube.

## Checking a scan {#scan.check}

Want to know if a scan is right? Press **Give me moves**. Start with a solved cube, white on top and green facing you, and do those moves. Then scan it. We'll tell you if the scan matches the cube you made.

## Camera picture {#settings.scan.flip}

Some cameras and webcam apps flip the picture left to right. For a cube with the usual colors, we can work this out from the colors by ourselves ("Work it out from the colors").

**Check left and right** finds out for sure: hold the cube up, and when we say so, move it to your right. We remember the answer for that camera on this device.

## Mirror picture {#settings.scan.mirror-preview}

"Show the picture like a mirror" flips only what you see on the screen, so moving the cube to your right moves it to the right on screen too. It doesn't change how we read the cube.
