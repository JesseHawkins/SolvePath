# M1: Cube core and 3D cube

Status: done, waiting for owner review.

## What's in it

**Cube model** (`packages/cube-core`, no DOM)

- **Sticker model:** a cube state is the list of 54 stickers in Kociemba facelet order (the layout most cube software uses, including the two-phase solver coming in M3). Centers are tracked too, so slices and whole-cube rotations are exact.
- **Moves are computed, not typed in.** Each move family says which axis it turns, which layers, and which way. The sticker permutation is built by rotating the 3D sticker positions. All 18 families: U R F D L B, wide moves (Rw or r), slices M E S, and rotations x y z, each with ' and 2.
- **Notation:** parsing uses cubing.js's parser, so groups, repeats, commutators, and conjugates all work (`(R U)2`, `[R, U]`, `[F: R]`). We accept only real 3x3 moves and give a clear error for anything else. Writing moves back out round-trips.
- **Cubie model:** corner and edge positions, twists, and flips, using Kociemba's names and rules. Converts to and from stickers with nothing lost.
- **Orientation:** all 24 ways to hold a cube, found by trying rotations (not typed in). Detects how a cube is held from its centers, and turns it back to the standard hold.
- **Validity diagnosis:** takes 54 sticker colors and lists every problem it finds (wrong color counts, impossible centers, unknown or repeated pieces, a twisted corner, a flipped edge, two swapped pieces), so the camera flow in M2 can tell people what to fix. Valid input comes back as a state, in the orientation it was held.
- **Point of view:** one tested function, `cameraGridToBackFace`, turns what the camera sees (raw or mirrored "selfie" image) into the user's B face. A test builds a pretend camera, projects every sticker into an image, and checks the result for thousands of random cubes.
- **Colors:** sticker colors, color schemes (Western by default), a scheme checker, cube profile type, and display colors.

**Testing the model**

- 55 cube-core tests, most of them property tests (fast-check) over random move sequences that use every kind of move: a sequence then its inverse is solved; stickers to cubies and back is lossless; every reachable state passes the validity check and is rebuilt exactly; orientation detection for all 24; point of view for raw and mirrored images.
- **Cross-check with cubing.js:** for 2,000 random sequences, our model and cubing.js's (completely separate) model agree on the position and twist of every corner, the position and flip of every edge, and the position of every center. The mapping between the two numbering schemes is learned from how moves behave, not typed in.
- Known facts check out: `R U R' U'` has order 6, `R U` has order 105, `M = R L' x'`, `r = R M'`, and so on.

**3D cube** (`apps/web/src/cube3d`)

- three.js 0.187.0 with react-three-fiber 9.8.2. 26 plastic pieces and 54 rounded stickers.
- Each turn plays as a short animation (150 ms quarter turn, faster when moves pile up, instant with reduced motion). The canvas only draws when something changes, to save battery.
- **Face highlight:** the turning layer stays bright and the rest of the cube dims.
- **Arrows:** an arrow on a visible face shows which way the next move turns (double head for half turns).
- **Swipes:** swipe a row or column to turn it; swipe the middle to turn a slice; swipe beside the cube to turn the whole cube.
- **Keyboard:** cubing.js's 3x3 key map (csTimer layout: J/F for U, I/K for R, and so on). The Guide's key table is checked against it by a test.
- State lives in a small Zustand store: the logic updates at once, and the 3D view catches up by playing a queue of turns, so fast typing never drops a move.

**Virtual cube page** (`/cube`, in the top menu)

- Swipe, keys, or turn buttons. Scramble (cubing.js random-state scramble), Undo, Reset. "Solved!" shows for a solved cube held any way.
- Type moves and press **Do them**, or **One at a time** to step through with the arrow and highlight.
- The cube page and three.js load only when someone opens it.

**Guide**

- New page: Virtual cube (`feature.virtual-cube`, `cube.controls`, `cube.tryMoves`).
- New glossary entry: Moves and notation.

## Changes after the owner's first review

- Turns animate again (they were finishing in one frame after the cube sat still) and are a bit slower: 280 ms for a quarter turn, with ease in and out.
- Whole-cube turns with a mouse: more room around the cube, plus right-drag or Shift-drag anywhere.
- Turn buttons in three labeled groups: one layer (including E and S), two layers (r, l, u, d, f, b), and the whole cube (x, y, z, each both ways). Whole-cube buttons have an accent outline.
- Whole-cube moves get a big curved arrow around the cube. Layer moves keep the straight arrow on the part that moves; two-layer moves get a wider one.
- The highlight follows the move's layers (both layers for r).
- New **Arrows** setting on the Cube page (saved on this device, with a Guide entry): **Section arrows** (straight, on the part that moves, the default) or **Face arrows** (a circle on the face that turns, for the six outer faces only). Two-layer moves, middle slices, and whole-cube turns keep their own arrows in both styles. Every circle lies flat on its own face. Faces you can't see from the normal view (L, D, B) also get the straight arrow.
- Half turns (R2, M2, x2, and so on) now animate the same way as their single turn, matching their arrows.
- **Inspect** button in the corner of the cube: drag to spin the view freely, with no snapping, to see any side. It changes only the view, so moves still use the normal view's faces. Arrows and highlights stay stuck to the cube, so they point at the part that will really turn. Press Inspect again (or Esc) and the view glides back.

## Numbers

- Unit tests: 150 (56 cube-core). Browser tests: 30 passed, 4 skipped (axe scans and mouse-button tests run on desktop only). Axe scans now cover the cube page and its Guide page in all three themes.
- Bundle: the cube page adds a 247 kB gzipped three.js chunk (three.js plus react-three-fiber) and 8 kB of page code. The rest of the app is unchanged.

## For the owner to try

1. Open **Cube** on this computer, Angela's iPhone 14 Pro, and your Pixel. Swipe some rows. Does it feel smooth, and does each swipe turn the row you meant?
2. Click the cube and try the keyboard (J, F, I, K, and so on).
3. Type `R U R' U'` and press **One at a time**. Do the arrows make sense?
4. Press **Scramble**, then try to solve it on screen if you like.

## Not in M1 (on purpose)

- `@react-three/drei`: not needed. The plan listed it for helpers we didn't end up using.
- Camera orbit (spinning the view freely): the view stays in your point of view, with F facing you, because that's what the solving guide will need. Whole-cube rotations (x, y) are real moves instead.
- Cube profile editing and storage arrive with the camera (M2) and profiles (M9).
