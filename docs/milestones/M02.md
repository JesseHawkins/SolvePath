# M2: Vision spike

Status: in progress.

## Done so far

### Owner request from M1 review: stronger highlight

When a move is highlighted (One at a time, and later the solving guide), the layers that will move now get:

- a thin accent-colored **frame** on the cube's surface around the moving band. It runs along the gaps between stickers, so it never hides a color, and
- a soft **halo** around the outside of those layers.

Both use the theme's accent color, so they work in every theme. They are steady (no blinking or pulsing), so they're fine with reduced motion. They hide while a turn is playing, so they never cut through moving pieces. The halo is left open where the moving layers meet the rest of the cube, so it doesn't spill onto parts that stay still. Whole-cube turns (x, y, z) get no glow, since their arrow already goes around the whole cube.

### Vision package (in progress)

- `packages/vision`: OKLab color math, images as plain data, the Hungarian algorithm (checked against brute force), and the face finder (grid fit). The face finder first looks for the evenly spaced "comb" of four lines a face makes, then nudges a 3x3 grid into place.
- `packages/vision/src/synthetic`: a small software renderer that draws a scrambled cube (stickered or stickerless) in a hand, with lighting presets, noise, and an optional flipped feed. It also scripts whole grip-and-spin scans, so tests always know the right answer.

Found so far:

- When the top is tipped toward the camera, a finger can hide more than the center sticker. So the assembler will search for the most likely **valid** cube piece by piece (Hungarian matching of corners and edges), and hidden stickers just count for less.
- Face finding works on synthetic stickerless and stickered faces. Telling "a face is held still" from "mid-spin" still needs a fair test (frames labeled by their true spin angle).
