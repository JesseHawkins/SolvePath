# M2: Vision spike

Status: **built; waiting for the owner.** Everything below works on synthetic video and passes CI. The go/no-go decision (PLAN.md 5.12) waits for real clips of the GAN cube and a test on the owner's phones.

## What changed

### Owner request from M1 review: stronger highlight

When a move is highlighted (One at a time, and later the solving guide), the layers that will move now get:

- a thin accent-colored **frame** on the cube's surface around the moving band. It runs along the gaps between stickers, so it never hides a color, and
- a soft **halo** around the outside of those layers.

Both use the theme's accent color, so they work in every theme. They are steady (no blinking or pulsing), so they're fine with reduced motion. They hide while a turn is playing, so they never cut through moving pieces. The halo is left open where the moving layers meet the rest of the cube, so it doesn't spill onto parts that stay still. Whole-cube turns (x, y, z) get no glow, since their arrow already goes around the whole cube.

### Vision pipeline (`packages/vision`, pure TypeScript, no DOM)

| File           | What it does                                                                                                                                                             |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `color.ts`     | OKLab color math (to and from sRGB), color distance that counts lightness less                                                                                           |
| `image.ts`     | Images as plain data, OKLab conversion of just the area we need, auto brightening for dark pictures                                                                      |
| `hungarian.ts` | The Hungarian algorithm (cheapest pairing), checked against brute force                                                                                                  |
| `grid.ts`      | Face finder: finds the "comb" of four evenly spaced lines a face makes, then nudges a 3x3 grid into place                                                                |
| `sample.ts`    | The 9 sticker colors of a found face (median of the inner half, shine dropped)                                                                                           |
| `motion.ts`    | Side-to-side motion between frames, for the spin direction                                                                                                               |
| `cluster.ts`   | Balanced color groups: exactly 9 stickers per color                                                                                                                      |
| `pieces.ts`    | The most likely **real** cube, piece by piece (corners and edges matched with the Hungarian algorithm, then the cube's three rules enforced)                             |
| `assemble.ts`  | Puts six scanned faces together: tries every spin direction, top turn, bottom turn, and hidden center color (up to 64 ways), keeps the best real cube                    |
| `session.ts`   | A grip-and-spin scan, frame by frame: waits for a face held still, captures it, counts sides, top, bottom                                                                |
| `calibrate.ts` | Left/right camera check: follows the moving, colorful cube                                                                                                               |
| `check.ts`     | Compares a scan with a known scramble, however the cube was held                                                                                                         |
| `synthetic/`   | Test camera: a software renderer that draws a scrambled cube in a hand (stickered or stickerless, four lighting presets, flipped feed), and scripted grip-and-spin scans |

### In the app

- **Scan Lab** (new page, "Scan Lab" in the top menu). Rough on purpose. Live grid on the camera picture (yellow while looking, green when steady), hints, captured faces, the scanned cube laid flat, and how long each picture takes to read.
- **Check the scan:** "Give me moves" shows a scramble. Do it on a solved cube, scan, and the page says whether the scan matches. This is how we measure real accuracy without labeling stickers by hand.
- **Camera picture setting:** work it out from the colors (default), normal, or flipped. **Check left and right** calibrates and remembers the answer per camera on this device. **Mirror picture** only changes what's shown.
- **Vision worker** (Comlink): each frame is shrunk to 270 pixels on its short side, moved to the worker, read once, and closed. 12 frames a second, never more than one waiting. Nothing is recorded, saved, or sent.

### Tools

- `npm run vision:report`: runs 16 synthetic scans (2 cube styles x 4 lighting presets x normal or flipped feed) plus every real clip, writes `docs/vision-accuracy.md`, and fails if fewer cubes are read right than the baseline (`packages/vision/accuracy-baseline.json`). CI runs it as its own job and installs ffmpeg for the real clips.
- `npm run fixtures:record`: dev-only recorder page (HTTPS, works from a phone on your Wi-Fi). Shows a scramble, records a clip, and downloads it with a facts file. The only code in the project that records video; it never ships in the app.
- `npm run fixtures:add -- <clip> <facts.json>`: checks the facts and copies the clip into `packages/test-fixtures/vision/`.

### Tests

- Unit tests: 179 (vision: 28, including full synthetic scans in three conditions and a flip test). Property tests for the Hungarian algorithm and piece matching (60,000 cases checked locally).
- Browser tests: 33 passed, 4 skipped, in both base paths. One uses a **fake webcam**: Chrome plays a synthetic scan of a known cube as its camera. The page reads it through the real path (camera, frame pump, worker) and gets the exact cube.
- Accuracy report: 16 of 16 synthetic scans read exactly right, about 10 ms per frame on a desktop.

## Results so far (synthetic only)

- Every synthetic scan reads the exact cube, including dim light, glare, warm light, stickered and stickerless cubes, and a flipped feed.
- Reading one frame takes about 8 to 10 ms on a desktop in Node. The budget on the iPhone 14 Pro is 25 ms. Scan Lab shows the real number on the phone.
